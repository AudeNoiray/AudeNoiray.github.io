## Margherita Belia

Margherita Belia is a postdoctoral researcher at the Laboratoire de Psychologie & Neurocognition (LPNC). She sees language learning as a holistic, embodied process, embedded in infants’ everyday experiences and shaped by continuous interactions among their motor, cognitive, social, and neurophysiological systems. 
 
During her PhD at the University of York, she studied the role of sleep in early lexical development, and the joint influence of sleep and locomotion on early vocabulary growth. She also examined how naps and nocturnal sleep support novel word learning in infants. In parallel, she was awarded funding to conduct a pilot study using ultrasound imaging of the tongue during vocalisation, eating, drinking, and rest in naturalistic contexts, in collaboration with Catherine Laing. This work strengthened her interest in the biomechanical foundations of speech and in understanding vocal development as embedded in a multifunctional system. 

**Research Experience**:

In her first postdoc with Louise Goupil, she investigated the emergence of vocal control and intentional communication in early infancy, focusing on how social interaction and vocal practice shape early communicative development. She also developed an interest in cortical speech tracking to study infant language processing. 
In her second postdoc within the VOC2SPEAK project, she integrates and extend these perspectives by examining how infants’ speech-related motor behaviours (vocal practice and respiration) shape neural processing of speech.
